Project Mission & Methodology

The History of Intervention is an independent, non-commercial, public historical reference project documenting foreign policy, military actions, covert operations, civilian consequences, and corporate relationships from 1945 to the present.

1. Editorial Principles

Historical honesty requires examining abuses of state and economic power. The project documents atrocities, political interference, civilian casualties, occupations, and forced displacement without softening the record due to the geopolitical prominence of the governments or corporations involved.

At the same time, rigorous scholarship requires five fundamental distinctions in every published record:

  1. Established historical events: Verified facts confirmed across multiple independent archives and participants.
  2. Documented government and corporate actions: Operational cables, official orders, signed contracts, and meeting minutes.
  3. Allegations that remain disputed: Claims supported by partial testimony or disputed forensic evidence.
  4. Historical interpretations: Differing analytical perspectives among accredited historians.
  5. Unresolved historical questions: Gaps where evidence remains classified, destroyed, or inconclusive.

2. Institutional Focus & Rejection of Collective Blame

Strict Institutional Accountability

The project focuses exclusively on institutions, state policies, command decisions, intelligence agencies, military units, and corporate directors. We firmly reject any characterization of Americans, Israelis, Jews, Arabs, Muslims, or any other civilian population as collectively responsible for the decisions or crimes of governments, armies, or corporations.

3. Source Verification Criteria

No fictional articles, unverified internet rumors, or partisan talking points are permitted. Entries must cite verifiable primary or peer-reviewed documentation:

  • Declassified Government Records: U.S. National Archives, Foreign Relations of the United States (FRUS), CIA FOIA Electronic Reading Room, UK National Archives.
  • Official Judicial and Parliamentary Inquiries: The Church Committee (1975), the Kahan Commission (1983), the MacBride Commission (1983), the 9/11 Commission (2004), the Chilcot Report / UK Iraq Inquiry (2016).
  • Academic Press Scholarship: Oxford University Press, Cambridge University Press, Columbia University Press, Stanford University Press.
  • International Judicial Bodies: The International Court of Justice (ICJ), International Criminal Court (ICC), UN Special Rapporteurs.

4. Privacy & Zero Tracking Policy

The website operates under a strict privacy-first architecture:

  • No user registration or accounts required.
  • Zero analytics, telemetry, or third-party tracking scripts.
  • Zero commercial advertising.
  • Bookmarks and theme preferences are stored strictly in the user's local browser via localStorage.
  • Designed to function 100% offline via modern Progressive Web App (PWA) standards.

5. Reporting Errors & Archival Inaccuracies

Historical accuracy is an ongoing collaborative commitment. If you discover a factual error, broken bibliographic link, or newly declassified document that impacts an existing record, please open an issue on the project's public GitHub repository or email the editorial maintainers:

GitHub Issues: https://github.com/fasad-usgov/fasad-usgov.github.io/issues

Editorial Correspondence: editorial@historyofintervention.org
