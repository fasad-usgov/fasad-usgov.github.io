# History of Intervention
## Global History, Civilian Consequences, and Corporate Accountability

### 1. Project mission

Build a free, public, offline-capable historical website that documents the foreign policy, military interventions, covert operations, and human consequences of the United States and Israel, beginning in the aftermath of World War II.

The project will primarily examine the Middle East, while also covering relevant events in Asia, Africa, Latin America, and Europe.

The website should help readers understand:

- What happened during major historical events.
- Why governments and other institutions became involved.
- What consequences these actions had for local populations.
- How governments, intelligence agencies, military organizations, and corporations contributed.
- What historical evidence exists, and what remains disputed.
- How individual events connect to larger historical developments.

The website is a historical reference resource, not a news aggregator, social media platform, or political campaigning tool.

Its purpose is to make important historical information accessible to people around the world.

### 2. Editorial direction

The project should be critical of abuses of power, including those committed by the United States and Israel.

It must not avoid documenting atrocities, civilian deaths, political interference, occupation, forced displacement, or other serious historical harms simply because powerful governments or corporations are involved.

At the same time, historical accuracy is essential.

The website must distinguish between:

1. Established historical events.
2. Documented government or corporate actions.
3. Allegations that remain disputed.
4. Historical interpretations and competing explanations.
5. Questions that available evidence cannot conclusively answer.

Do not manufacture a balance between well-established facts and unsupported denial.

Do not treat every historical disagreement as equally credible.

Do not portray Americans, Israelis, Jews, Arabs, Muslims, or any other population as collectively responsible for the actions of governments, organizations, or individuals.

Focus on institutions, decisions, policies, actions, and their consequences.

### 3. Technology requirements

Build a static Progressive Web App using:

- HTML5.
- Vanilla CSS.
- Vanilla JavaScript.
- JSON for structured historical content.
- A service worker for offline functionality.
- A web app manifest for installation.
- GitHub Pages for free initial hosting.

Avoid unnecessary frameworks and dependencies.

Do not use React, Vue, Angular, Next.js, Tailwind CSS, or a backend server for the initial release.

The site must work on desktop computers, mobile phones, and tablets.

It should remain usable when the visitor has no internet connection.

Use semantic HTML, responsive CSS, and native browser APIs wherever possible.

### 4. Website structure

Create the following main sections.

#### A. Home

The homepage should introduce the project and provide immediate access to the historical collection.

Include:

- Project mission.
- Search bar.
- Featured historical events.
- Historical timeline.
- Regional navigation.
- Corporate accountability section.
- Recently added historical entries.
- Browse by decade.
- Browse by country.
- Browse by topic.

The homepage should feel like the entrance to a digital history book.

Avoid sensational headlines, excessive animation, autoplay videos, and distracting advertisements.

#### B. Historical timeline

Create a chronological timeline beginning in 1945.

Visitors should be able to browse events by:

- Decade.
- Year.
- Region.
- Country.
- Historical topic.
- Government or institution involved.

The timeline should connect related events.

For example, readers exploring the 1953 Iranian coup should be able to discover related events involving Iran, the United States, the United Kingdom, and subsequent developments in Iranian history.

Every event must have its own permanent URL.

#### C. Historical event pages

Every major historical event should have a dedicated page.

Each page should contain:

1. Event title.
2. Date or historical period.
3. Geographic location.
4. Summary.
5. Historical background.
6. Main actors and institutions.
7. Chronological account.
8. Documented consequences.
9. Civilian and societal impact.
10. Corporate involvement, where applicable.
11. Historical significance.
12. Related events.
13. Further reading and viewing.

Provide a clear distinction between the main historical account and interpretations of the event.

Do not turn every page into a long essay.

Use a concise introduction followed by expandable sections for readers who want more detail.

#### D. Countries and regions

Create dedicated regional and country indexes.

Initial regional categories:

- Middle East.
- North Africa.
- Sub-Saharan Africa.
- South Asia.
- East Asia.
- Southeast Asia.
- Latin America.
- Europe.

Include country pages that connect historical events, related institutions, and corporate involvement.

The Middle East should receive priority during the initial research and publication phases.

#### E. Corporate accountability

Create a dedicated section examining companies involved in historical military, intelligence, political, and economic activities.

Potential categories:

- Arms manufacturers.
- Oil and energy companies.
- Military contractors.
- Technology and surveillance companies.
- Communications and infrastructure companies.
- Financial institutions.
- Companies involved in sanctions enforcement or resource extraction.

Document the actual role of each company.

For every corporate entry, explain:

- Company name and historical identity.
- Relevant dates.
- Country of operation.
- Nature of its involvement.
- Government agencies or organizations involved.
- Documented financial or material relationships.
- Consequences of its activities.
- Relevant historical events.
- Available supporting sources.

Distinguish direct participation from indirect commercial relationships.

Do not assume that a company's involvement in one historical event proves involvement in another.

Include historical company names, mergers, subsidiaries, and relevant corporate successors.

#### F. Research library

Create a searchable bibliography of books, documentaries, academic research, journalism, declassified documents, and other published historical material.

Readers should be able to find sources by:

- Author.
- Title.
- Publication date.
- Historical period.
- Country.
- Topic.
- Source type.

Include a dedicated page for each important source.

Do not reproduce entire copyrighted books, documentaries, articles, or photographs.

Provide bibliographic information, brief descriptions, and links to legitimate sources.

### 5. Initial historical research priorities

The first release should prioritize a carefully researched collection of major historical events rather than attempting to cover every intervention immediately.

Use the following as the initial research roadmap.

#### Phase A: Early Cold War and Middle East

Investigate:

- The 1948 Arab-Israeli war and the Palestinian displacement.
- The 1953 Iranian coup.
- The 1956 Suez Crisis.
- The 1967 Arab-Israeli war.
- The 1973 Arab-Israeli war.
- The 1978–1979 Camp David negotiations and their regional consequences.
- The 1979 Iranian Revolution and subsequent US-Iran relations.
- The 1980–1988 Iran-Iraq War.
- The 1982 Israeli invasion of Lebanon.
- The 1982 Sabra and Shatila massacre and subsequent investigations.
- The 1990–1991 Gulf War.

#### Phase B: Post-Cold War and the War on Terror

Investigate:

- The 1991 Gulf War and its aftermath.
- The 1990s Iraq sanctions regime.
- The 2001 invasion of Afghanistan.
- The 2003 invasion of Iraq.
- The occupation of Iraq and its consequences.
- The 2006 Lebanon war.
- The 2008–2009 Gaza war.
- The 2011 intervention in Libya.
- The 2011 Syrian uprising and subsequent international involvement.
- The 2015 Saudi-led intervention in Yemen.
- The 2018–2020 US-Iran escalation.
- The 2023 Gaza war and its historical context.

The dates and scope of each entry must be checked during research.

Do not treat the above list as an exhaustive account of either country's foreign policy.

#### Phase C: Global historical coverage

After the Middle East collection is established, expand into:

- US involvement in the 1954 Guatemala coup.
- The Vietnam War.
- The 1973 Chilean coup.
- The 1965–1966 Indonesian mass killings and foreign involvement.
- US interventions and covert operations in Central America.
- US military involvement in Afghanistan before 2001.
- US involvement in the Balkans during the 1990s.
- Relevant Israeli military, intelligence, diplomatic, and commercial activities outside the Middle East.

Include other countries and institutions when they are relevant to the historical record.

### 6. Research and source collection

The agent must prioritize reputable historical material.

Recommended source categories:

**Academic research**
- University press books.
- Peer-reviewed historical research.
- Academic journals.
- Established research institutes.

**Historical documentation**
- Declassified government documents.
- Official inquiry reports.
- Parliamentary investigations.
- Court records.
- International organization reports.

**Journalism**
- Established investigative journalism.
- Newspaper archives.
- Documentary reporting.
- Interviews with participants and witnesses.

**Documentaries**
- Historical documentaries.
- Investigative documentary films.
- Interviews and recorded testimony.

Do not treat documentaries as automatically reliable.

Check their claims against other available evidence.

#### Research workflow

For each historical event:

1. Establish the historical question.
2. Identify the relevant dates, locations, actors, and institutions.
3. Gather reputable books, articles, reports, and documentary material.
4. Identify primary documents where available.
5. Compare the major historical accounts.
6. Extract the important facts.
7. Identify unresolved disputes.
8. Write the historical narrative.
9. Record the sources used.
10. Review the entry for accuracy and completeness.

The final website should present the historical account, not the internal research process.

Do not include fabricated citations, invented quotations, or sources that have not been examined.

### 7. Historical content format

Use JSON files for the historical content.

Suggested structure:

`data/events/`

`data/countries/`

`data/companies/`

`data/sources/`

`data/topics/`

Each event should have a stable identifier.

Example:

```json
{
  "id": "iran-coup-1953",
  "title": "The 1953 Iranian Coup",
  "date_start": "1953-08-19",
  "date_end": "1953-08-19",
  "region": "Middle East",
  "countries": ["Iran"],
  "topics": [
    "Cold War",
    "Covert Operations",
    "Political Intervention"
  ],
  "summary": "",
  "background": "",
  "timeline": [],
  "actors": [],
  "consequences": [],
  "corporate_involvement": [],
  "related_events": [],
  "sources": [],
  "last_updated": ""
}
```

The example is a structural template, not a complete historical article.

Use the same structure consistently across the collection.

Avoid embedding large images or documentary files directly into JSON.

### 8. Website design

Create a clean, readable historical reference interface.

The design should emphasize:

- Typography.
- Clear historical timelines.
- Readable long-form text.
- Simple navigation.
- Accessible contrast.
- Responsive layouts.
- Fast page loading.
- Minimal distractions.

Use a restrained historical visual identity.

Avoid designs that imitate government propaganda, military recruitment material, or sensationalist news websites.

Use maps, photographs, archival documents, and timelines only when they add meaningful historical context.

Every image must have appropriate attribution and alternative text.

Provide captions explaining the historical relevance of important images.

### 9. Search and navigation

Implement client-side search using vanilla JavaScript.

The search system should support:

- Historical event titles.
- Countries.
- Companies.
- Dates.
- Topics.
- Authors and sources.

Allow filtering by region, decade, country, and subject.

Search should remain functional offline using the locally available historical dataset.

Use simple URL patterns, such as:

`/events/iran-coup-1953/`

`/countries/iran/`

`/companies/`

`/sources/`

Ensure that important pages can be bookmarked and shared.

### 10. Progressive Web App requirements

The website must be installable as a PWA.

Implement:

- `manifest.webmanifest`
- `service-worker.js`
- Application icons.
- Offline support.
- A responsive application shell.
- An offline status indicator.
- A clear update notification when new content is available.

Use a cache-first strategy for static assets.

Use a network-first strategy for content updates where appropriate.

Cache the initial historical dataset so the main collection remains accessible offline.

Do not cache external websites or third-party video platforms.

When offline, show a clear message that external resources may be unavailable.

Use the browser's native storage APIs to save user preferences and bookmarks locally.

Do not require account registration.

For technical implementation, follow the official [MDN Progressive Web Apps documentation](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Progressive_web_apps), including its guidance on installation, manifests, and offline functionality.

### 11. Copyright and media

The project must respect copyright.

Do not copy complete articles, books, documentaries, or other copyrighted works into the website.

Instead:

- Write original historical summaries.
- Provide bibliographic references.
- Link to original publications.
- Use short quotations only when appropriate.
- Attribute photographs and archival materials.
- Respect licenses and usage restrictions.

Prefer public-domain material and appropriately licensed media.

Use legitimate video embeds only when the source permits embedding.

### 12. Performance and accessibility

The website must:

- Load quickly on mobile connections.
- Support keyboard navigation.
- Use semantic HTML.
- Include accessible labels.
- Provide readable typography.
- Avoid unnecessary JavaScript.
- Work without a JavaScript framework.
- Avoid large dependencies.
- Remain usable on lower-powered devices.

Do not autoplay audio or video.

Do not make essential historical content dependent on animations.

### 13. Security and privacy

This project does not need user accounts, advertising, analytics, or a backend server for its initial release.

Do not collect personal information.

Do not include third-party tracking scripts.

Do not expose API keys or credentials.

Do not add comment sections or user-generated historical claims during the initial release.

Provide a simple contact mechanism for reporting factual errors or broken references.

### 16. Definition of done

The first release is complete when:

- The website is publicly accessible.
- It works on desktop and mobile.
- It can be installed as a PWA.
- Its main historical collection is available offline.
- Search works without an internet connection.
- Historical events have permanent URLs.
- Sources are attached to historical claims.
- Corporate involvement is documented where relevant.
- Copyright requirements are respected.
- No backend server is required.
- The project can be maintained using basic HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and JSON.

The initial release should prioritize a reliable historical foundation over the number of published articles.

A smaller collection of well-researched events is more useful than a large collection of superficial summaries.

### 17. Final instruction to the coding agent

Build the website as a maintainable, open-source historical reference project.

Prioritize the Middle East and the historical record of US and Israeli foreign policy, military actions, covert operations, and their consequences.

Include the relevant roles of companies, government agencies, intelligence organizations, and other institutions.

Use reputable historical research and published documentation.

Keep the implementation simple, lightweight, accessible, and suitable for free static hosting.

Do not add a complex content management system, backend, or unnecessary framework.

Do not generate fictional historical articles to fill the website.

Build the complete website foundation first, then populate it with researched historical content in a structured and maintainable way.
