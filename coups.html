US-Led Coups, Regime Change & The Imperial Playbook

Drawing upon investigative journalism (Johnny Harris’s Every US-Led Coup, Mapped), primary archival releases (Church Committee, FRUS, National Security Archive), and classic historiographical studies (Stephen Kinzer’s Overthrow, Lindsey O'Rourke’s Covert Regime Change).

The Core Definition: A coup d’état is an unconstitutional, illegal seizure of power removing a sovereign head of state or government without popular democratic consent. This dossier traces how the United States transitioned from territorial expansion to mastering covert regime change—subsidizing military conspirators, deploying disinformation, and using economic warfare to topple governments that challenged Western multinational corporations or foreign policy doctrines.

The Anatomy of a US Coup: The 4-Step Playbook

Across Latin America, the Middle East, Africa, and Asia, declassified CIA post-mortems reveal a remarkably consistent operational sequence:

Stage 1: Economic Warfare

"Make the economy scream": The US cuts off foreign credit lines, freezes central bank assets, boycotts nationalized commodities (sugar, oil, copper), and finances domestic trucker and merchant strikes to manufacture artificial shortages and middle-class panic.

Examples: Chile 1970–73, Guatemala 1953–54, Iran 1951–53.

Stage 2: Information Warfare

Psychological Operations: The CIA buys local newspaper editors, plants sensational disinformation, broadcasts propaganda on pirate transmitters (Radio Swan, Radio Liberación), and finances provocateurs to stage violent clashes blamed on the government.

Examples: Guatemala (PBSuccess), Iran (Operation Ajax).

Stage 3: Cultivating Generals

The Military Ultimatum: Clandestine officers deliver cash disbursements, tactical radios, and explicit White House security guarantees to senior army generals, providing "kill lists" and ensuring that loyalist resistance is neutralized.

Examples: Brazil (Brother Sam), Indonesia (1965), South Vietnam (1963).

Stage 4: Restitution & Dictatorship

Corporate & Geopolitical Payout: Washington grants emergency diplomatic recognition within hours. The new military junta dissolves congress, bans trade unions, privatizes national resources, and restores concessions to American multinationals.

Examples: United Fruit, Freeport Sulphur, ITT, British Petroleum.

Three Epochs of American Regime Change

1893–1945

The Corporate Frontier

Direct corporate coups in the Americas and Pacific. Private sugar barons, banana monopolies (United Fruit, Cuyamel), and railroad syndicates recruited mercenaries and petitioned the US Navy and Marines to unseat uncooperative sovereigns in Hawaii, Nicaragua, and Honduras.

1947–1979

The CIA Golden Age

Following the National Security Act of 1947, covert regime change became institutionalized statecraft. Under Allen Dulles and Henry Kissinger, covert ops toppled democracies in Iran, Guatemala, Congo, South Vietnam, Brazil, and Chile, frequently installing military dictatorships.

1980–Present

Proxy Wars & Overt Invasions

Transition from clandestine surgical coups to vast proxy wars (Chad, Afghanistan), conventional direct invasions (Grenada 1983, Panama 1989, Iraq 2003), and multilateral aerial campaigns (Libya 2011), often leaving collapsed states in their wake.

1893 Pacific • Hawaii

Overthrow of the Hawaiian Kingdom

A cabal of 13 white American sugar and pineapple tycoons (led by Sanford Dole and Lorrin Thurston) conspired with US Minister John L. Stevens to overthrow Queen Liliʻuokalani. 162 armed US Marines landed from the USS Boston to intimidate loyalists, paving the way for annexation.

Blowback: Extinction of Native Sovereignty; 1993 US Congressional Apology
1909 Central America • Nicaragua

Ouster of José Santos Zelaya

Nicaraguan President José Santos Zelaya resisted American canal concessions and negotiated with Japan/Britain. Secretary of State Philander Knox (former counsel for US mining companies) issued the "Knox Note" demanding his resignation and dispatched US warships, installing General Juan Estrada.

US Mechanism: Gunboat diplomacy, Knox Note, landing of 400 Marines.
Blowback: Decades of continuous US military occupation (1912–1933) and the rise of the Somoza family dictatorship.
1911 Central America • Honduras

The Cuyamel Fruit Coup (Banana Wars)

Banana tycoon Sam Zemurray (Cuyamel Fruit Company / United Fruit) financed mercenary general Lee Christmas and armed the gunboat Hornet to overthrow President Miguel Dávila after Dávila refused exclusive tax and land concessions.

Corporate Actor: Cuyamel Fruit & United Fruit Company.
Outcome: Replaced by Manuel Bonilla, who granted Zemurray 24,700 acres of prime tariff-free land, birthing the term "Banana Republic."
1953 Middle East • Iran

Operation Ajax (TPAJAX)

The CIA and British MI6 orchestrated the overthrow of democratically elected Prime Minister Mohammad Mosaddegh following his nationalization of the Anglo-Iranian Oil Company (now BP). CIA operative Kermit Roosevelt hired street mobs, bribed military commanders, and restored the autocratic Shah.

Blowback: 1979 Iranian Revolution & 40+ Years Hostility
1954 Latin America • Guatemala

Operation PBSuccess

CIA covert operation overthrowing democratically elected President Jacobo Árbenz after he enacted Decree 900 land reforms expropriating uncultivated United Fruit Company holdings. Secretary of State John Foster Dulles and CIA Director Allen Dulles held substantial stock and legal ties to United Fruit.

Blowback: 36-Year Civil War & 200,000+ Dead (Maya Genocide)
1960–1961 Central Africa • DR Congo

Assassination of Patrice Lumumba

CIA Director Allen Dulles designated elected Prime Minister Patrice Lumumba an "urgent and prime objective." The CIA dispatched biological toxins, funded Colonel Joseph Mobutu's coup, and assisted in delivering Lumumba to a Belgian-supervised firing squad in Katanga to secure Shinkolobwe uranium and copper reserves.

Blowback: 32 Years of Mobutu Kleptocracy & Regional Wars (5M+ Dead)
1961 Caribbean • Dominican Republic

Assassination of Rafael Trujillo

Long-time brutal dictator Rafael Trujillo, once an ally of Washington, was assassinated by dissident military conspirators who were covertly supplied with firearms and operational support by the CIA, as confirmed in the 1975 Church Committee report.

US Mechanism: CIA clandestine arms drops, diplomatic distancing.
Outcome: Subsequent 1965 direct US military invasion (Operation Power Pack) to block the return of democratically elected leftist President Juan Bosch.
1963 Southeast Asia • South Vietnam

Assassination of Ngo Dinh Diem

Frustrated by the Buddhist Crisis and authoritarian paralysis, the Kennedy administration authorized Cable 243, instructing Ambassador Henry Cabot Lodge Jr. and CIA operative Lucien Conein to greenlight an ARVN generals' coup against President Ngo Dinh Diem, resulting in his execution.

1964 South America • Brazil

Operation Brother Sam

Overthrow of reformist President João Goulart by right-wing generals. President Lyndon B. Johnson ordered a US Navy aircraft carrier battle group, tear gas, and ammunition to Brazilian shores. Installed a 21-year military dictatorship that institutionalized torture and inspired Operation Condor.

1965–1966 Southeast Asia • Indonesia

Purge of Sukarno & The Jakarta Method

General Suharto sidelined anti-imperialist founding President Sukarno, using covert US communication equipment, funding, and US Embassy execution lists containing thousands of names to exterminate an estimated 500,000 to 1,000,000 suspected leftists and trade unionists.

1973 South America • Chile

Project FUBELT & Fall of Allende

Violent military overthrow of democratically elected socialist President Salvador Allende on September 11, 1973. Directed by Richard Nixon, Henry Kissinger, the CIA, and ITT Corporation. General Augusto Pinochet established a 17-year dictatorship characterized by concentration camps, torture, and Operation Condor.

1982 Sahel / Central Africa • Chad

CIA Backing of Hissène Habré

To "bloody Muammar Gaddafi's nose" in neighboring Libya, the Reagan administration and CIA covertly funneled weapons and cash via Sudan to warlord Hissène Habré, toppling the government of Goukouni Oueddei. Habré's secret police (DDS) subsequently slaughtered an estimated 40,000 political opponents.

US Agency: CIA Station Khartoum & Reagan Administration.
Outcome: Habré convicted of crimes against humanity and torture by a special African tribunal in 2016.
1983 Caribbean • Grenada

Operation Urgent Fury

Following the internal coup and execution of socialist Prime Minister Maurice Bishop by a rival military faction, President Ronald Reagan deployed nearly 2,000 US troops to invade the Caribbean island, citing the protection of American medical students and eliminating Cuban construction engineers.

US Mechanism: Direct conventional military airborne and amphibious assault.
Blowback: Condemned by the UN General Assembly (108–9) as a flagrant violation of international law.
1989 Central America • Panama

Operation Just Cause (Invasion of Panama)

President George H.W. Bush deployed 27,000 US troops to invade Panama and overthrow former CIA asset and military dictator General Manuel Noriega. Intense aerial and artillery bombardment leveled the El Chorrillo neighborhood, killing hundreds to thousands of civilians before Noriega was captured and extradited.

Blowback: UN General Assembly Condemnation & 2018 OAS Liability Ruling
1979–1992 Central / South Asia • Afghanistan

Operation Cyclone & Fall of Najibullah

The largest covert operation in CIA history, funneling over $6 billion in arms and cash through Pakistan's ISI to radical Islamist Mujahideen commanders against Soviet forces and the government of Mohammad Najibullah, which collapsed in 1992.

Key Actors: CIA, Pakistan ISI, Charlie Wilson, Osama bin Laden's Maktab al-Khidamat.
Blowback: Devastating civil war, rise of the Taliban in 1996, sanctuary for Al-Qaeda, and the 2001–2021 US War in Afghanistan.
2002 South America • Venezuela

The 2002 Coup Attempt Against Hugo Chávez

A short-lived military coup kidnapping elected President Hugo Chávez and installing business chamber chief Pedro Carmona. The Bush administration had funded opposition groups through the NED and immediately endorsed the coup government before popular uprisings and loyalist soldiers restored Chávez.

Operation Unified Protector & Gaddafi Ouster
